Leafix GHY / Guwahati Itinerary

3 days in Guwahati.
A practical itinerary.

Guwahati is the gateway to the Northeast. Here's how three days here actually play out — without the tourist-brochure version.

The itinerary

Three days. Temples, river, markets, rhinos.

Day 1

Arrive. Settle. See the Brahmaputra.

  • Arrive

    Check in to Leafix GHY

    Self check-in — door code sent ahead. Settle, drop bags, rest. No reception desk, no waiting.

  • Afternoon

    Fancy Bazaar & Uzan Bazar

    Old-city Guwahati. Dense, noisy, full of character. Cotton University area is good for a walk. Paan stalls, old buildings, chaotic lanes.

  • Evening

    Brahmaputra riverfront at sunset

    Uzan Bazar ghat. Sit on the steps as the river gets golden. One of the best free things to do in Guwahati. Ferries cross to North Guwahati if you're curious.

  • Dinner

    Local Assamese meal near Fancy Bazaar

    Dal, rice, pork or fish curry, aloo pitika. Cheap and very good at the small spots in the lanes.

Tip: If your train or flight arrives early, go straight to the riverfront before it gets crowded. Mid-morning is better than afternoon for the Brahmaputra light.

Day 2

Kamakhya Temple. Then the city.

  • Early AM

    Kamakhya Temple

    One of the most significant shakti temples in India — built on the Nilachal Hill, overlooking the Brahmaputra. Go before 8am on a weekday to avoid the longest queues. The view from the hill alone is worth the trip.

  • Midday

    Umananda Temple (river island)

    A small Shiva temple on a tiny island in the Brahmaputra. Reached by a short ferry from Uzan Bazar ghat. The island itself is scenic. Gives you another reason to be on the river.

  • Afternoon

    Assam State Museum

    Near Dighalipukhuri Park. Good collection covering Assam's history, tribal culture and archaeology. Not huge — a couple of hours at most.

  • Evening

    Pan Bazaar or GS Road for dinner

    More restaurant options on GS Road — Korean, Chinese, Assamese, North Indian. Pick your pace. Pan Bazaar has street food if you'd rather keep it cheap.

Avoid Kamakhya on Tuesdays, Saturdays or during festivals — queues can be multiple hours. Monday or Wednesday mornings are usually fastest.

Day 3

Day trip to Pobitora. Or a slower morning and departure.

  • 5:30 AM

    Pobitora Wildlife Sanctuary (day trip)

    About 45 minutes from Guwahati. One of the highest concentrations of one-horned rhinoceros in the world. Jeep safaris start at first light. Back in Guwahati by noon. Good for early departures in the afternoon.

  • Alternatively

    Sualkuchi — Assam's silk village

    About an hour from Guwahati across the Brahmaputra bridge. Famous for Muga and Eri silk weaving. The weaving workshops are visible from the road. Buy directly from weavers.

  • Or

    A slower morning — chai, notebook, the balcony

    Not every day needs a plan. Leafix GHY has a kitchen for breakfast and enough quiet that a morning of doing very little still counts as a good morning.

  • Departure

    Check out and onward

    Airport 25–30 min. Railway Station ~15 min. Self checkout — no reception to hand keys to.

Common questions

Things people ask before visiting Guwahati.

How many days do you need in Guwahati?

Two to three days covers the main sights. A third day works well as a day-trip day — Pobitora for rhinos, or Sualkuchi for silk. If Guwahati is a transit point on your way to Meghalaya, Assam tea gardens or Kaziranga, two nights is usually enough.

When is the best time to visit Kamakhya Temple?

Early morning on a weekday — before 8am — for the shortest queues. Avoid Tuesdays, Saturdays and festival periods.

Is Pobitora Wildlife Sanctuary worth visiting from Guwahati?

Yes, if you have half a day. It's about 45 minutes out, and seeing rhinos in the wild at that proximity is unusual. Go early — the animals are more active in the morning and you get better light.

What is Guwahati known for?

Kamakhya Temple, the Brahmaputra river, Assamese food (pork, fish, rice, aloo pitika), being the gateway to the Northeast, and a particular kind of old-city character that places like Uzan Bazar still carry.
